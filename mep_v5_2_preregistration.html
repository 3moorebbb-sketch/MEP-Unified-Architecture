Executive Summary

The V5.1 Crucible on Split-CIFAR10 resulted in catastrophic failure (0.0% retention) because the Jacobian Topological Tether was applied to the raw input pixels. This constrained the shallowest layers but allowed the deep hierarchical Convolutional filters to scramble. V5.2 hypothesizes that moving the tether into the Latent Feature Space will lock the deep convolutional geometry and prevent catastrophic forgetting on complex tasks.

1. The Hypothesis & Mechanism

We hypothesize that Catastrophic Forgetting in deep Convolutional Neural Networks can be mitigated by calculating the Jacobian-Vector Product (JVP) of the Latent Feature Space (the output of the final convolutional layer) with respect to the input pixels. This anchors the deep feature extractors ($E(x)$) while allowing the final linear classifier to dynamically adjust.

2. Experimental Design

  • Benchmark: Split-CIFAR10 (Task A: Classes 0-4; Task B: Classes 5-9).
  • Network Architecture: Deep Latent CNN.
  • Strict Memory Budget: Both the Baseline and the MEP algorithm are granted an exact memory budget of $N=50$ data points extracted from Task A.

3. Baselines & Seeds

  • Baseline: Experience Replay (ER) using the 50-point buffer.
  • Target Model: MEP Latent Space Tether (JVP penalty applied to both the Latent Space and Output Space).
  • Seeds: 10 independent random initializations.

4. Metrics & Statistical Adjudication

The primary metric of success is the Harmonic Mean of Task A Retention Accuracy and Task B Acquisition Accuracy.

HarmonicMean = 2 × (Retention_A × Acquisition_B) / (Retention_A + Acquisition_B)

5. Explicit Failure Conditions (Falsifiability)

Failure Conditions

The MEP Latent Space Tether hypothesis will be considered FAILED if:

  1. Baseline Equivalence: The MEP architecture's Harmonic Mean fails to outperform the Experience Replay baseline by a statistically significant margin ($p \ge 0.05$).
