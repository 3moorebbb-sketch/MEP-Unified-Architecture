Engineering Constraints

Open Problems & Fabrication Roadmaps

Translating this stochastic map into physical "Smart Dust" operating near the Landauer limit presents profound engineering challenges. This outlines the primary physical hurdles for future materials science research.

1. The Power Budget: Micro-TEG Density vs. Optical Pumping

The Constraint

Real-world TEGs at the micro-scale yield power densities in the tens to hundreds of nanowatts. Conversely, maintaining a continuous-wave "Echo Memory" loop requires continuously pumping a photonic cavity. Even aggressive resonators with $Q \sim 10^6 - 10^7$ demand pump power in the microwatt to milliwatt range. The gap is orders of magnitude wide.

Research Pathways

Initial unit cells must abandon the "Smart Dust" form factor. Deploying larger chips attached directly to high-$\Delta T$ industrial environments (e.g., server exhaust) can mathematically close the power budget, pending breakthroughs in ultra-high-Q passive structural nonlinearity.

2. Phase Decoherence in the Langevin Regime

The Constraint

High levels of thermal noise natively cause phase diffusion. At ambient temperatures, the required noise levels threaten to scramble the wavelength-multiplexed phase tags, destroying orthogonal isolation and causing state decoherence.

Research Pathways

The architecture must rely heavily on the structural robustness of the fractal attractor basins to act as a discrete thresholding mechanism—forcing the wave to snap into the nearest stable state, effectively shedding minor thermal phase jitters. Cryogenic cooling may be required for initial optical loops.
