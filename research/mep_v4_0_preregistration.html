The Hypothesis

The failure of V3.3 was caused by the discrete nature of PyTorch layers (ReLUs acting as digital shears). By replacing discrete layers with a Continuous-Time Neural ODE, the phase-multiplexing of data ($e^{i\pi/2}$) is integrated smoothly as a continuous boundary flow. This continuous physical dynamic will prevent "phase leakage" and allow Task A (Real) and Task B (Imaginary) to co-exist orthogonally in the same network geometry without catastrophic forgetting.

1. Experimental Design

  • Benchmark: Split MNIST (Task A: Digits 0-4; Task B: Digits 5-9).
  • Network: MEPContinuousSubstrate (An Isomorphic 2D mapping running on the dopri5 Runge-Kutta ODE solver).
  • The Operation:
    1. Train on Task A (Initial ODE Boundary Condition: purely Real vector).
    2. Apply the rotation matrix $\mathcal{P} = e^{i\pi/2}$ to swap the Real and Imaginary axes of the initial state.
    3. Train on Task B (Initial ODE Boundary Condition: purely Imaginary vector).

2. Baselines & Seeds

  • Naive Real Neural ODE: A standard continuous ODE that trains sequentially on Task A then Task B without the isomorphic complex dimension or phase rotation. This tests if the ODE solver alone prevents forgetting, or if the Phase rotation is doing the work.
  • Seeds: 20 independent random initializations.

3. Explicit Failure Conditions (Falsifiability)

Failure Conditions

The Continuous Phase-Multiplexing hypothesis will be considered FAILED if:

  1. Phase Shear: The MEP Neural ODE with Phase Rotation retains less than 85% relative accuracy on Task A. (If the continuous non-linearity $\tanh$ still causes the imaginary gradients to leak into the real axis, the mathematical theory cannot be digitized).
  2. Baseline Equivalence: The MEP Neural ODE with Phase Rotation fails to outperform the "Naive Real Neural ODE" by a statistically significant margin ($p < 0.05$).
